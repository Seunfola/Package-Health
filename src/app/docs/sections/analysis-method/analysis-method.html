Analysis, accuracy & mathematics

DepVault is a multi-signal decision aid. It obtains registry metadata, download data, maintainer metadata, release timestamps, license information, and known-vulnerability data; normalizes those signals across ecosystems; then produces a score, a vulnerability list, and—when enabled—a deterministic uncertainty interval. It does not inspect your application code or prove a dependency is safe.

The score model

The base score is bounded to 0–100 and combines four normalized sub-scores:

Score = 0.45 × Security + 0.20 × License + 0.20 × (Maintenance × Freshness) + 0.15 × Popularity + Diversity adjustment
  • Security: CVSS base scores are summed into vulnerability burden B. A category prior w adjusts the Poisson rate: λ = B × (1 + w). The security score is 100 × P(X ≤ t | λ), where t is the configured risk tolerance. High attack-surface categories use w = 0.30, medium 0.15, and low 0.05.
  • Freshness: uses a true half-life: freshness = 2^(−days / halfLife). The default half-life is 365 days, so the factor is 0.5 at one year and 0.25 at two years.
  • Maintenance and popularity: are mapped to bounded scores from maintainer count and download volume. Maintenance is then multiplied by freshness, so stale packages cannot retain the full maintenance contribution.
  • License: evaluated as an SPDX expression, not a string match. Permissive scores 100, weak copyleft 60, strong copyleft and commercially restrictive terms 0, and a missing licence is treated as unknown rather than permissive. The "or later" suffix (GPL-3.0+, AGPL-3.0+) and compound expressions are handled properly: a disjunction such as MIT OR GPL-3.0 takes its safest operand because you may choose it, while a conjunction such as MIT AND GPL-3.0 takes its worst because you are bound by both.
  • Diversity: measures maintainer concentration with normalized Shannon entropy, H = −Σ pᵢ log₂(pᵢ). A normalized diversity value below 30 receives a 15-point penalty. Public email providers are treated as individual maintainers, not one shared organization.

Unavailable signals are excluded, never invented

When a signal cannot be obtained, its weight is redistributed across the signals that are known, and the gap is reported through data quality and confidence — it is never substituted with a constant. Two distinct cases exist:

  • Transient — a rate-limited or unreachable registry. Counted against the score's confidence.
  • Structural — the ecosystem has no such concept. Go module proxies expose no owners, download counts or licence, so those terms are excluded rather than scored as zero, and they do not count toward the "too many unknowns" cap that would otherwise pin every Go module below a passing grade.

This is why a report distinguishes scored low from could not be verified. A dependency nobody could check is a finding, not an absence — the summary counts it separately rather than dropping it.

Hard-stop conditions

The final score becomes 0 when vulnerability burden exceeds 20, or when a package is demonstrably abandoned — freshness below 0.15 where a real publish date exists, or zero maintainers where the ecosystem actually reports maintainers. The qualifiers matter: a missing date is not evidence of abandonment, and an ecosystem that has no maintainer concept must not be failed for it. These safeguards stop a high score in unrelated dimensions masking an extreme risk signal, without punishing a package for metadata its registry never publishes.

Confidence interval and accuracy

When Monte Carlo confidence is enabled, DepVault runs 1,000 reproducible simulations seeded from the lockfile hash. Each simulation perturbs severity by ±20% and downloads by ±10%, then recomputes the score. The displayed 95% interval is the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of those results. It expresses sensitivity to this defined input-uncertainty model—not a statistical guarantee that a package is secure, and not a probability that an exploit will occur.

Accuracy principles: known vulnerability data is shown as findings rather than hidden inside a score; unknown or unreachable registry data is reported as unverified rather than turned into a fabricated low score; and the seeded simulation gives identical inputs identical confidence bounds in CI. Registry freshness, advisory coverage, metadata quality, and the qualitative category prior remain real limitations.

Where DepVault adds value

A conventional vulnerability audit answers whether its advisory database currently knows about a vulnerable version. DepVault retains that answer, then adds maintenance, freshness, license, popularity, diversity, category criticality, confidence bounds, and workflow enforcement across browser, CLI, editor, MCP, and CI. This makes it useful for prioritizing otherwise clean-looking packages and for applying a consistent policy before install or merge.

No unsupported superiority claim: DepVault is not documented as universally more accurate than every scanner. Its advantage is broader decision context and consistent enforcement surfaces. Use advisory IDs, package compatibility tests, and your security process alongside every Trust Score.
